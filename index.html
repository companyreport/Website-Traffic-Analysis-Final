The read

This is the first period where growth came from the right place. Views rose 41% and visitors only 14%. Working the arithmetic: 231 visitors at the old depth of 1.19 would have produced 274 views. The site produced 340. Of the 99 additional views over last period, roughly 66 came from deeper reading and 33 from more people arriving. For three reports the advice has been to fix the internal linking so readers go past the page they land on. Something moved.

Two days carried the depth: Tuesday the 20th at 2.00 views per visitor and Thursday the 22nd at 1.96. Both roughly double the site's recent baseline, and both well clear of anything in the last month. On the 20th, 48 visitors read 96 pages. That is the pattern of people landing on an article and working through related pieces — the behaviour the compliance cluster and the news hub were built to produce.

Three new articles entered the top six and took 17% of all site traffic between them. The Davis-Bacon wage-floor piece at 24 views, the consultant-fee warning at 19, and the Navy faked-test breaking story at 16 — 59 views for three pieces that did not exist a week ago. All three are compliance and enforcement. Seven reports running, every single one has seen new work in that cluster break into the top ranks. It is no longer a pattern worth testing; it is the thing this audience comes for.

The blog hub doubled. News & Insights went from 9 views to 18, a 100% rise. In the report where reading depth broke its plateau, the page whose job is to send readers onward doubled. Those two facts belong together, and they point at the hub as the mechanism rather than a coincidence.

Both money pages rose with the tide. The Consulting service page took 32 views (+19%) and Discovery Call 28 (+17%). Neither is back to the highs of late September, but both moved in the right direction while the new articles pulled traffic in — which suggests the articles are routing people forward rather than absorbing them.

Search recovered 32% and Facebook 15%. Search went 59 to 78, Facebook 39 to 45. Last report every channel fell together and the read was that fewer things were pointing at the site. This period they rose together, which supports that diagnosis: the problem was distribution, and distribution improved.

LinkedIn doubled off the floor, which still leaves it broken. 3 views to 6. Across seven reports the sequence is 22, 7, 7, 7, 3, 6. Six views is not a recovery from 22 — it is noise around a channel that stopped working five weeks ago and has not been addressed.

Publishing stopped on Wednesday and traffic held. Posts landed Monday (8), Tuesday (7) and Wednesday (5), with the remainder of the 27 most likely on Friday. Yet Thursday delivered 55 views at 1.96 per visitor with nothing new published that day. The library is carrying weight between drops, which is what a maturing catalogue looks like.

The weekend gap widened again. Saturday 15 and Sunday 24 against a weekday average of 60 — a ratio of 3.1 to 1, up from 2.0 last report. Weekends remain small. Nothing published then is being seen.

Attribution slipped slightly. Untracked traffic rose from 55.6% to 59.1%. After three reports of steady improvement this is the first step backwards, and it is small — but it means almost three in five views still have no identifiable source.

773 subscribers. Seventh consecutive report, unchanged. The site has gone from 104 posts to 248 across this series — 144 more posts — and the subscriber count has not moved a single time. This period the site finally fixed the engagement problem that had been flagged for three reports. The capture problem has been flagged for seven and remains untouched.

What to do next

Ordered by what unblocks the most.

  1. Work out what made October 20 and 22 different, and repeat it2.00 and 1.96 views per visitor against a baseline of 1.2. These are the two best depth days in six weeks and they are the evidence that the internal linking can work. Open those days in Jetpack, see which article people landed on and where they went next, and apply that structure to the rest of the library.
  2. Fix the subscriber capture — seventh report, 144 posts, zero subscribersEverything else on this page improved. This has never moved. Open a live post on a desktop and on a phone, confirm a subscribe form renders and submits, and send a test address through to GoHighLevel. The email report showed that list's main problem is address quality; the site is your one source of clean, self-selected addresses and it has captured none.
  3. Build the compliance hub page properlyDavis-Bacon, consultant fees and the Navy faked-test story took 17% of all traffic in their first week. Seven reports have now confirmed this cluster. Give it a single hub page, cross-link every enforcement and compliance piece into it, and link that hub high in each new article — that is the structure that produced the two high-depth days.
  4. Stop publishing into Saturday and SundayWeekday average 60 views, weekend 20. A 3.1-to-1 gap, and it has been present in every report in this series. Anything that lands Friday evening through Sunday is being dropped into an empty room. Shift that output to Monday through Thursday.
  5. Test the article template at phone widthMobile rose 25% to 21.8% of visitors. Still a minority, but one reader in five now arrives on a phone. Check where the subscribe prompt and the CTAs land on a small screen before the next template change.
  6. Connect Search Console and GA4Search recovered 32% and you cannot see which queries or pages drove it, nor whether the earlier fall was a ranking change. Nearly three in five views still arrive untracked. Seventh report carrying this item.

Brick by brick.
